# Summary

Thank you for all the valuable feedbacks on the manuscript. We have carefully considered each comment and made the necessary revisions to improve the clarity and quality of the paper. Below is a summary of the changes made in response to your feedback:

- **Expanding the scope of models and data**: Originally we only considered the YOLOv8n model, which is the lightest model in the YOLOv8 family. However, it did not show consistent performance across different datasets. In this revision, we have included the latest YOLOv11n model, as well as its larger variant, YOLOv11m that have nearly ten times more parameters than YOLOv8n (2.6 million vs. 20.1 million). A Real-Time DEtection TRansformer (RTDETR) model is also included, which integrates the attention mechanism into the detection process, providing an efficient approach to learn the contextual relationships between objects in the images. To the dataset, we acknowledge that the lack of external dataset could limit the generalizability of our findings. Therefore, we have included several images from the iNaturalist dataset to show the limitation of this study. 
- **Sliding window approach**: We have extended the sliding window approach to include the official SAHI library, which is well-recognized implementation library for this technique to ensure the robustness and reliability of the method. Furthermore, we included several optimization strategies, including grid search and Bayesian optimization, to provide a detailed guide for readers on how to effectively implement this approach in their own work.
- **Domain knowledge in ant foraging and computer vision**: We recognize the diverse readership of the Ecological Informatics journal, where readers may not have a background in ant species or computer vision. To address this, we have added several sentences across the manuscript to bridge those gaps and provide a clearer understanding of the concepts discussed. We also emphasized that the focus of this paper is not to create a novel CV algorithm, but rather to demonstrate how existing algorithms can be effectively applied to ant foraging or other ecological problems that require long-term monitoring of small objects by images.
- **Practical computational cost**: We also carefully re-run the experiements to measure the required computational resources, such as GPU memory and time in model calibration and inference. We have included these details for those who are interested in the practical aspects of implementing the proposed methods in their own research.
